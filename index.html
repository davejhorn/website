Selected work

Three problems, and what changed.

CDW · 2025–present Owned and built

An agentic intake pipeline that replaced a human bottleneck

The problem. Every new technology request ran through a single business relationship manager — capture, vetting meetings, sizing, business case. One person was the sole coordinator and the bottleneck, and the round trip took weeks. Then the role was eliminated.

What I built. Five agents and skills covering the whole path: an Idea Intake Agent for conversational capture, a Team Identifier that predicts impacted teams and splits scope, a Sizing Estimator, an ROI Calculator, and a One-Page Creator that produces the leadership-ready business case. I owned the roadmap and built the agents.

What changed. Pipeline time fell by half — three weeks out of the process. Task time dropped 75% across roughly 45 demands a month. The agents absorbed seven FTEs of work, about $1.3M a year. When adoption stalled at 20% after beta, I proposed retiring the legacy intake form and carried it through leadership approval; every technology demand now enters through the agent.

CDW · 2025–present Built and led

Armada: turning AI capability into an organizational habit

The problem. Tools alone do not change how an organization works. Capability was concentrated in a handful of people, and the rest of the portfolio had no route in.

What I built. An AI enablement program and community of practice — roughly 20 dotted-line contributors working through structured delivery cycles, supported by hands-on training, guest sessions, reusable prompts, office hours, and co-development cohorts. Alongside it, a centralized agent catalog, a standardized status taxonomy, and executive reporting on adoption and realized value.

What changed. Roadmap priorities became live solutions rather than proposals, and AI tooling reached all 120 delivery teams in Technology. Governance moved from ad hoc to visible: leadership could see what existed, what it was worth, and what came next.

Motorola Solutions · 2013–2022 Product Manager

Nine years owning mission-critical products

The problem. A large portfolio of internal and customer-facing products serving public-safety and enterprise customers, where investment had to be justified against value, feasibility, and real delivery capacity — and where failure carries consequences beyond revenue.

What I built. Prioritization and backlog management for a $150M portfolio in Jira and Aha!, direct strategy and development ownership of a $24M product portfolio, and a decision practice grounded in Tableau dashboards, revenue forecasting, voice-of-customer research, and competitive analysis. I led the cybersecurity and compliance work that kept products aligned to industry standards.

What changed. Full lifecycle ownership from discovery and roadmap advocacy through execution, across nine years and a SAFe Agile operating model — the foundation everything since has been built on.
